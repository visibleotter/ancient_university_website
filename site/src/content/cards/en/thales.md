**Who he was.** Thales lived in Miletus, a rich Greek city on the coast of today's Turkey. He was counted first among the "Seven Sages". He is said to have traded, travelled, visited Egypt and brought geometry back from there. According to Herodotus he predicted a solar eclipse, which astronomers date to 28 May 585 BC (how he could have done it is unknown ❓).

**What geometry is credited to him** (by Eudemus via Proclus, except the last item):

1. [A diameter bisects the circle](../diameter-bisects/).
2. [The base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal](../isosceles-base-angles/).
3. [Vertical angles are equal](../vertical-angles/) — ✅ Eudemus is named.
4. [A triangle is fixed by one side and the two angles at it](../asa-ships/) — used to find the distance of ships at sea; ✅ Eudemus is named.
5. [The angle in a semicircle is a right angle](../angle-in-semicircle/) — 📜 according to Pamphila, 1st century AD.

And also [measuring a pyramid by its shadow](../pyramid-shadow/) 📜.

**What matters.** Not *what* Thales knew (the Egyptians knew a great deal) but that the story of **proof** begins with him: a statement is not just noticed but explained. According to Proclus, he approached some things "more generally" and others "more visually".

👶 **A story for little ones.** Thales was so busy looking at the stars that he fell into a well! A servant girl laughed: "He wants to know what is in the sky but cannot see what is at his feet." We will wonder at things too, but we will look both at the sky and at our feet.
